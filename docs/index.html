Safety

Careful with your work.

A cleaner you can’t trust is worse than a full disk. These rules are built into every delete.

  • Nothing goes without review.You see every item and its size before anything is removed.
  • The Trash comes first.Folders, files and apps move to the Trash, so you can put them back.
  • Source code is off limits..git, .env, your home folder and iCloud folders are never offered.
  • Checked again before deleting.Every item is re-verified a moment before removal, not trusted from an old scan.
  • Running projects are left alone.If a dev server is using a folder, DevSweep says which one and skips it.
  • Nothing leaves your Mac.No accounts, no analytics, no network requests. Privacy

Questions

Is it safe to delete node_modules and build folders?

Yes. They are rebuilt by your package manager or build tool. DevSweep shows how to rebuild each one, skips projects with something running, and moves everything to the Trash first.

Why isn’t it on the Mac App Store?

App Store apps run in a sandbox that can’t see other apps’ caches or your project folders, which is exactly what a cleaner needs. So DevSweep is downloaded directly, like most developer tools.

Why does macOS warn me the first time?

macOS shows that message for apps downloaded outside the App Store that aren’t notarized yet. Clicking Open Anyway once in Privacy & Security lets it run, and it opens normally from then on.

What does Full Disk Access let it do?

Measure folders macOS protects, like Library, Mail, Photos and the Trash. DevSweep reads sizes and names; it doesn’t open your documents or messages.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

Yes. It’s a universal app that runs natively on Apple silicon and Intel, on macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

How do I uninstall DevSweep?

Quit it from the menu bar, move it to the Trash, and delete ~/Library/Application Support/DevSweep. That’s everything it stores.
