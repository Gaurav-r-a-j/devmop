Opening DevMop the first time

If macOS says it can’t verify DevMop, click Done, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to DevMop, then enter your Mac password. You only do this once.

Full Disk Access

macOS protects Library, Mail, Photos and the Trash. To let DevMop measure them:

  1. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Full Disk Access.
  2. Turn on DevMop, or click + and choose it in Applications.
  3. Switch back to DevMop. It notices and scans again.

After updating DevMop, if access stops working, turn it off and on again in that list.

Troubleshooting

A project says it’s “in use”

Something is running inside it, such as a dev server. The message names the process. Stop it in Processes & Ports, then clean again.

The pnpm store still looks big after pruning

Pruning only removes packages no project uses. DevMop remembers the size after pruning and only shows what’s been added since.

Some items were “left in place”

Read-only, locked or other users’ files are never forced. Everything else in that item was removed.

Freed space doesn’t show up right away

Items in the Trash still use space until it’s emptied, and macOS can take a little while to release space held by Time Machine local snapshots.

Start fresh

Quit DevMop and delete ~/Library/Application Support/DevMop. Your next scan starts from scratch.

Uninstall DevMop

  1. Quit DevMop from its menu bar icon.
  2. Turn off Open at login in its Settings, and remove it from Full Disk Access.
  3. Move DevMop from Applications to the Trash.
  4. Delete ~/Library/Application Support/DevMop and ~/Library/Preferences/app.devmop.DevMop.plist.

Still stuck?

Report a problem on GitHub or email YOUR-EMAIL. Include your macOS version, your Mac (Apple silicon or Intel) and what you expected to happen.
